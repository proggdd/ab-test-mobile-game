What looked like the cost

Early retention drops for a full week before the curves meet again. My first readout called that churn and held the release. It is not churn — the next section shows why.

Whether players left needs no cohort anchor at all. The count of active days per player sits in the published user level file, and it answers the question directly.

The cumulative shares converge by day seven. The disengaging segment holds 7,528 players in A and 7,541 in B — the same group, to within four hundredths of a point. Inside it, mean active days are 4.21 against 3.26. B removes nobody. It makes the same players stop about a day sooner, and those players have three to four active days out of fifty-nine.

Day 14 differs by 0.08 points, day 28 by 0.00, no player in either arm has zero active days, and daily actives trend upward in both arms across the whole window. Reproduce it with python analysis/reaudit_active_days.py in the repository — standard library only, runs on the committed data.

The call

My first readout held the release. Revenue was up, early retention was down, and trading a durable asset for a measurable one looked like a bad deal. The re-audit removed the durable asset from the equation: the same share of players disengages either way, and B only changes when the lowest-value segment stops.

What remains is a nine percent revenue gain with identical long-run retention. Across the window the treatment arm earned about twenty-six thousand dollars more. There is no measured cost to set against it.

1
Ship it in stages

Use day 14 and day 28 retention as the stopping rules, not day 1. Day 1 moves here for reasons that do not predict survival.

2
Re-anchor the retention curve

Rebuild the cohort metric on install date rather than first active day in the window, and censor mid-window installs. The conclusion holds without it, but the curve should be right.

3
Keep an eye on the early segment

Check whether players who now stop at day three instead of day five carry residual value. On this dataset they do not, but that is a property of the data, not a law.
